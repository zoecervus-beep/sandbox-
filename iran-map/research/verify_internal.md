# Verification of research/internal.json

Adversarial fact-check of the de facto records inside modern Iran (c. 1800-2026). Every low/medium-confidence record and every self-flagged item was re-checked against Encyclopaedia Iranica (read directly where the site could be reached), India Office files on the Qatar Digital Library, FRUS, journal and monograph-based summaries, and, as a cross-check only, Wikipedia. Grokipedia was not used. Edits were made in `research/internal.json`: it is valid JSON, the schema is unchanged, no region has time-overlapping records, every start/end event reference resolves, and `build/build.py` still runs with no new warnings (only the 8 pre-existing frontier-overlap warnings).

**Totals:** 37 change lines = 35 modified de facto records, 10 new de facto records, 4 modified regions and 7 new regions, 8 modified events, 4 new events and 1 renamed event. De facto records 107 -> 117, regions 60 -> 67, events 94 -> 97. `frontier.json` was not touched.

**Most important corrections**

- Soviet occupation of Khorasan ended in March 1946, not 'winter 1943' (Soviet communique of 25 Feb 1946 and Kennan's 25 Mar 1946 telegram, FRUS 1946 VII doc. 282); Semnan province split out because it was evacuated at the same time.
- Qashqai: 1926-29 was direct rule (military governors), the 1929 war lasted about four months (late April to August), and 1929-33 'contested' becomes 1929-04..1929-08 contested plus 1929-08..1932 indirect.
- Kalhor: the 1800 'indirect' baseline is dropped; Davud Khan's autonomous mastery is c.1900 to 1911/12.
- Salar al-Dowleh held the Kermanshah-Kurdistan area until July 1912 (Farman Farma's expedition), not Dec 1911.
- Mahabad: breakaway administration starts 15 Dec 1945 (Iranica), not 22 Jan 1946; Mukri 'indirect' end 1880 -> 1883.
- Russian evacuation of Azerbaijan after Turkmenchay: most of it by about April 1828 but Khoy held to about Nov 1828.
- Wrong status words fixed: Sarhad (none -> indirect from c.1900), Mehran 1980-82 (continuous occupation -> contested), Ubeydullah 1880 (none -> contested), Hamadan Aug-Dec 1916 (contested -> Ottoman occupation).
- Omissions added: pro-German Gendarmerie rule at Shiraz (1915-16), the Zanjan Babi enclave (1850-51), Russian garrisons at Urmia/Khoy/Salmas (1910-18), British Abadan (1914-18); South Persia Rifles garrisons re-dated per town.

## 3. Still uncertain

1. Hamadan and Kermanshah, July 1916-March 1917: Ottoman entries (Kermanshah 2 July, Hamadan 10 Aug 1916) are solid, but the Russian return to Hamadan (Dec 1916 per Wikipedia citing Caven) and the Ottoman evacuation of Kermanshah (Feb-March 1917, after Ali Ihsan was recalled to Baghdad) are low-confidence; no primary source reached. Salar al-Dowleh's capture of Hamadan in 1911-12 has no date and no record.
2. Mehran 1980-86: contested status is inferred from Wikipedia's 'third time' (May 1986); the dates of the first Iranian recaptures are unknown. The 1988 MEK/Iraqi phase (Forty Stars, 18 June 1988) and its end (Mersad, late July) are Wikipedia-only.
3. Iraqi-held pockets 1988-90: UNIIMOG speaks of a few disputed positions (Iraq left 23 of 29 by Jan 1991); the press figure of 1,000 sq mi (2,600 km2) and the strip geometry are unverified. Iraq's June 1982 'withdrawal' also left some border land (Muslim ibn Aqil, Oct 1982, recovered about 150 km2); not mapped.
4. WWII zones: the Soviet zone (Azerbaijan, Gilan, Mazandaran, Gorgan, Qazvin, Semnan, north-east Khorasan) is well supported, but the British zone beyond Khuzestan, Ilam, Kermanshah, Lorestan, Hamadan and the Gulf coast (Fars, Isfahan, Kerman, Zahedan, Hormozgan) was not established; Sanandaj was added at low confidence. Whether Zanjan province and Ardabil were fully under the Azerbaijan People's Government is unverified (Iranica names Tabriz, 'all major routes into the province' and the Qazvin block).
5. Mashhad/Khorasan: Iranica ('Khorasan xi') says the Russians sent additional troops to occupy 'most of the territory' of Khorasan in WWI and that Britain amassed a 'very visible presence' after 1917 (East Persia Cordon, Malleson mission); no record exists for either, and the Russian garrison of Mashhad after the March 1912 shelling of the shrine is also unmapped.
6. Placeholder end dates still unknown: Qa'enat/'Alam (1935), Sistan/'Alam (1935), Tabas (1935), Bastak (1925), Mukri (1883), Baluchistan/Sarhad after 1928 (Iran governed through appointed sardars; unmapped). Tabas and Bastak were still hereditary around 1910-19.
7. Sarhad 1850-1900 may have been 'none' rather than 'indirect'; the status change rests on one Gazetteer of Persia passage (c.1908-10). Persian Baluchistan after 1928 may have been 'indirect' through the 1930s-40s (Wikipedia 'Dost Mohammad Khan Baloch'; Koyagi 2021 not re-read).
8. Kalhor: the c.1900 start and the 1911 versus 1912 date of Davud Khan's death are approximate; Kermanshah's other tribes (Sanjabi, Guran, Jaf) have no record.
9. Shahsevan 1884-1923 'none' versus 'indirect': Iranica says 'virtually independent' without describing allegiance. Luristan 'none' before about 1907 is also arguable.
10. Qashqai 1980-82 'contested' probably overstates a mobile insurgency; Turkmen Sahra 1979-80 likewise. Both are low-confidence and could be dropped.
11. Omani lease of Bandar Abbas (1793-1868) and the Chabahar record are coded foreign_occupation although they were leases/appanages; Chabahar's pre-1872 Omani presence was nominal.
12. Date uncertainties that were not resolved: day of Khiabani's rising (5 or 7 April 1920); Tabriz taken by the Democrats on 21 Nov or by 10 Dec 1945; Ottoman entry into Tabriz on 8 June, 18 June or 23 Aug 1918 (Wikipedia pages disagree) and into Urmia on 27 or 31 July 1918; Boir Ahmad Tamradi casualties (about 400 or over 1,000); British evacuation of Bushehr (mid-1857 per Iranica, Oct 1857 per Wikipedia); Gendarmerie coup at Shiraz on 10 Nov 1915 (British file) versus 'late December' (Iranica).
13. Possible further omissions, not mapped because under a month or too small or undated: the Babi stand at Shaykh Tabarsi (Dec 1848-May 1849, one fort) and the Neyriz rising of 1850; Lahak Khan Bavand's mutiny at Bojnurd/Quchan (July 1926); Bicaranlu's seizure of Shirvan and Quchan (Feb 1942); Mohammad Ali Shah's 1911 landing; Kerman and Yazd under pro-German gendarmes in 1915-16 (Wikipedia, undated); the Khamseh confederation under the Qavam family (arguably indirect); British East Persia Cordon posts from Zahedan to Birjand and Mashhad (1916-20) outside Sistan; Ottoman-Arab force near Ahvaz in March 1915.
